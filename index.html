About

I am a graduate student at North Carolina State University (NCSU) researching computational methods for solving particle transport problems on HPC systems. I work in the nuclear engineering department, under the guidance of Dr. Dmitriy Anistratov. My research is focused on hybrid methods, combining stochastic and deterministic methods through low-order equations to achieve efficient variance reduction in transport solutions. I am also a developer of MC/DC, a highly portable Python-based transport code that runs at scale on LLNL supercomputers such as Tuolumne, Quartz, and Dane. MC/DC was started by the Center for Exascale Monte Carlo Neutron Transport (CEMeNT), a DOE PSAAP-III center and is now being extended to include charged particle transport under the Center for Advancing the Radiation Resilience of Electronics (CARRE), a PSAAP-IV center. I was an active member of CEMeNT and currently am in CARRE; my specific contributions are in domain decomposition, weight windows, and other hybrid methods development.

I am originally from Albuquerque, so the land of enchantment will always be special to me. In particular the beautiful hiking, climbing, and green chile rellenos.

Variance reduction

Hybrid weight windows for time-dependent Monte Carlo

An automatic weight window method for global variance reduction in time-dependent problems. Window centers come from an auxiliary hybrid MC/deterministic low-order second-moment (LOSM) solve, discretized to second order in space and time. Efficiency is measured by figure of merit and relative error against an analytic benchmark.

Hybrid methods

Two-level macro–micro transport methods

The transport solution is split into a deterministic macro component and a Monte Carlo micro component. Current work extends the framework with quasi-Monte Carlo sampling and maximum-entropy closures. This is the core of my dissertation, Hybrid Stochastic–Deterministic Methods Based on Coupled Moment Equations for Linear Transport Problems.

HPC

Domain decomposition at scale

I designed and prototyped MC/DC's domain decomposition algorithm. It removed the memory bottleneck that limited problem size in massively parallel runs and enabled simulations on LLNL supercomputers at up to 512 nodes. I've also applied dynamic mode decomposition to Monte Carlo tallies to predict the flux for weight window generation.
